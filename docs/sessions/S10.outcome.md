# S10 outcome, 2026-09-07

Shipped, in `packages/stats`: Stryker 9.6.1 with the Vitest runner (`pnpm mutation`,
`stryker.config.json`, `break: null` for two weeks from today, then observed minus 5);
`test/sticky.test.ts`, the serial-correlation property test with the degradation and
"how much more N" tables pinned in the test names; `detectShift` (`src/shift.ts`), a
two-sided CUSUM on block medians with a dominance guard, tested on synth with planted
shifts; and the README with formulas, constants, failure modes, and a worked example. 68
tests. The local mutation score is 88.4% (412 mutants, 360 killed, 39 survived, 9 uncovered; 5 minutes at concurrency 6). The two-week `break` should land around 83.

What the brief got wrong: Stryker 10 needs Node 22.18 (a Babel transitive dependency);
this machine and the repo's `engines` say 22+, so it is pinned to 9.6.1. `packages/normalize`
does not exist yet, so the config covers stats only; S13 adds it. "Simple CUSUM on median
price over time" is not simple on an A/B ladder: the block median of a near-uniform ladder
is a coin flip, and the first honest calibration gave 46 to 92% false alarms on *stable*
uniform, 50/50 and 10/45/45 ladders. The stub now measures how dominant the median is inside
its blocks and answers `inconclusive: "no_dominant_price"` below 70%; on dominant-tier
ladders it has 0 false alarms in 200 seeds and places a 10% move at the exact block in 198.
The return shape grew an optional `inconclusive` field for that; `{ shifted, at? }` is
unchanged otherwise.

What was harder than expected: the "how much more N" answer is not one number. A common
floor is barely affected by stickiness (20 to 30 observations either way); a 10% floor goes
from 50 i.i.d. to 180 at stickiness 0.9 over 10 observers, and at stickiness 1 it is never
confirmed with 10 or 20 observers because the cell holds at most `observers` independent
draws. The wrong-tier rate above threshold goes from 0.5% i.i.d. to 8.8% (0.9 / 10) and
22.7% (1.0 / 10). The floor is still never below the truth. The real fix is counting distinct
panelists per tier, which needs `panelistId` in the input: flagged for S11 (dedupe per
panelist in the query endpoint before `resolve`) or S14. Not done here, deliberately: it is a
resolver change and the brief asked for robustness measurement, not a new rule.
